# Golden 015 — indirect byte and half-word operands: `*Roffset:B/UB/H/UH`

This trace fixes Table 1-21's four indirect byte and half-word modes —
`0x8` (`*Roffset:B`), `0x9` (`*Roffset:UB`), `0xA` (`*Roffset:H`) and
`0xB` (`*Roffset:UH`) — as *destinations* and as *sources* (printed
p. 1-25). p. 1-27 fixes the two-step resolution and the offset rule, and
p. 1-4 fixes the width's alignment and the big-endian byte lanes: the
byte at a word address is bits 31:24, the half-word at an even offset is
bits 31:16 or 15:0. The write's `addr` is the pointer's value and its
`mask` is the byte-mark set of the width, so a byte or half-word store
merges into the containing word rather than replacing it.

The image is 18 words = 72 bytes; the program is 12 instructions and all
12 retire.

## Program

All instructions are three-parcel `MOV` (opcode `0x06`) except the
five-parcel `$data` at `0x0012` and the `NOP` at `0x0046`. The first
parcel is `10 | opcode[5:0] << 8 | smode << 4 | dmode`; `D` is
`Roffset:W`, `8`/`9`/`A`/`B` are the indirect byte and half-word modes,
and `E` is `*Roffset:W` (Table 1-21 p. 1-25). The 16-bit operand fields
are unscaled byte offsets (p. 1-21 scales only the five- and ten-bit
fields).

## Retirement lines

Cycle accounting: `MOV` is 1 in Table 4-32 (p. 4-3). The p. 4-1
assumptions exclude indirect operands, so no Table 4-37..4-40
operand-access delay is added and the duplicated indirect rows of
Tables 4-38 and 4-40 are not double-counted (`sem/README.md`, choice 22).
No SP/MSP changes occur.

L06. `HRI pc=0022 insn=86d80010 sp=00000100>00000100 msp=00000180>00000180 we=1 addr=00000120 data=00ab0000 mask=04 trap=0 cyc=1`

Cycles: 1 — `MOV` min=max=1 (Table 4-32, p. 4-3).

Encoding: `86d8` = smode `0xD` (`Roffset:W`), dmode `0x8` (`*Roffset:B`); source offset `0x0010` (`0x110`), destination offset `0x0008` (Table 1-21 p. 1-25).

Transition: the destination pointer at `0x108` is `0x121`, so the byte store is at `0x121`; `0x121` is lane 2, so the byte marks are `0x04` and the data word is `0x00ab0000` (p. 1-4, `sem/README.md`'s mask convention). Merging into the word read at `0x120` gives `0x11ab3344`. The record's `addr` is the pointer's value `0x120`, not the stack slot `0x108`.

L07. `HRI pc=0028 insn=86fdcdef sp=00000100>00000100 msp=00000180>00000180 we=1 addr=00000110 data=ffffcdef mask=0f trap=0 cyc=1`

Cycles: 1 — `MOV` min=max=1 (Table 4-32, p. 4-3).

Encoding: source `0xcdef`, destination `0x0010`. The 16-bit field is sign-extended (p. 1-23), so the value is `0xffffcdef`.

Transition: `0x110` becomes `0xffffcdef`, the half-word to be stored at L08.

L08. `HRI pc=002e insn=86da0010 sp=00000100>00000100 msp=00000180>00000180 we=1 addr=00000120 data=0000cdef mask=03 trap=0 cyc=1`

Cycles: 1 — `MOV` min=max=1 (Table 4-32, p. 4-3).

Transition: the pointer at `0x10c` is `0x122`, so the half-word store is at `0x122`. The low half is at offset 2 of the word, so the marks are `0x03` and the data word is `0x0000cdef` (p. 1-4). Merging into `0x11ab3344` gives `0x11abcdef`.

L09. `HRI pc=0034 insn=86ed0004 sp=00000100>00000100 msp=00000180>00000180 we=1 addr=00000110 data=11abcdef mask=0f trap=0 cyc=1`

Cycles: 1 — `MOV` min=max=1 (Table 4-32, p. 4-3).

Encoding: `86ed` = smode `0xE` (`*Roffset:W`), dmode `0xD`; source offset `0x0004`, destination `0x0010` (Table 1-21).

Transition: the pointer at `0x104` is `0x120`; the word there is the merge of L06 and L08, `0x11abcdef`, which is written at `0x110`. This line proves both byte and half-word stores merged rather than replaced: either one alone would leave a different word.

L10. `HRI pc=003a insn=869d0008 sp=00000100>00000100 msp=00000180>00000180 we=1 addr=00000110 data=000000ab mask=0f trap=0 cyc=1`

Cycles: 1 — `MOV` min=max=1 (Table 4-32, p. 4-3).

Encoding: `869d` = smode `0x9` (`*Roffset:UB`), dmode `0xD`; source offset `0x0008`, destination `0x0010` (Table 1-21).

Transition: the pointer at `0x108` is `0x121`; the byte there is `0xab`, and `:UB` zero-extends it (p. 1-28) to `0x000000ab`. The `:B` form of the same byte would give `0xffffffab`, so this line discriminates the two.

L11. `HRI pc=0040 insn=86bd000c sp=00000100>00000100 msp=00000180>00000180 we=1 addr=00000110 data=0000cdef mask=0f trap=0 cyc=1`

Cycles: 1 — `MOV` min=max=1 (Table 4-32, p. 4-3).

Encoding: `86bd` = smode `0xB` (`*Roffset:UH`), dmode `0xD`; source offset `0x000c`, destination `0x0010` (Table 1-21).

Transition: the pointer at `0x10c` is `0x122`; the half-word there is `0xcdef`, and `:UH` zero-extends it to `0x0000cdef`. The `:H` form would sign-extend it to `0xffffcdef` (as L07's immediate already shows), so this line discriminates the two.

## Manual ambiguities and the choices they forced

- The byte marks are the big-endian bus convention (bit 3 is the byte at the word address, bits 31:24), which is the HRI contract (`sem/README.md`, choice 11; p. 1-4's Figure 1-2). A right-aligned convention would make L06's mask `0x01` and its data `0x000000ab`, and L08's mask `0x0c`; this trace is built so that mistake cannot pass.

- The pointed-to address obeys only the width's alignment (p. 1-4): `0x121` is legal for a byte, `0x122` is legal for a half-word, and the *offset* in each instruction is word-aligned as p. 1-27 requires. A model that confused the two rules would refuse L06 and L10.

- A byte or half-word store merges into the word it lands in; the record still carries one architectural word write with the access's byte marks and the value in its lanes (`sem/README.md`'s mask convention). L09's read-back is the evidence that the merge happened: the word is `0x11abcdef`, which is neither L06's nor L08's value alone.

- The 16-bit `$data` at L07 is sign-extended (p. 1-23), so the value written at `0x110` is `0xffffcdef`; only its low half is stored at L08, which is why L08's data word is `0x0000cdef` and L11's unsigned read is `0x0000cdef`.
